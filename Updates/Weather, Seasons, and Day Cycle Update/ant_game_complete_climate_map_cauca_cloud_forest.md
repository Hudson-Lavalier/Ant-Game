# Ant Game — Complete Climate Map
## Fictional Cloud-Forest Plateau, San Antonio / KM18, Valle del Cauca, Colombia

**Document status:** Climate research + game-design basis  
**Purpose:** Establish a real climatological foundation for the game's Day/Night, weather, humidity, rainfall, and seasonal systems before implementation decisions are made.

---

# 1. Recommended Canonical Setting

The game should be based on a **fictional broad mountain bench / plateau-like flat area** within the **San Antonio–KM18 cloud-forest landscape** on the Western Cordillera above the Cauca Valley near Cali, Colombia.

Recommended fictional site:

- **Region:** San Antonio / KM18 cloud forest, Valle del Cauca
- **Mountain system:** Western Cordillera of the Colombian Andes
- **Slope orientation:** Cauca-facing / eastern side of the range
- **Approximate elevation:** **1,900–2,000 m above sea level**
- **Biome:** Andean cloud forest / very humid lower montane forest
- **Topography:** locally broad, relatively level mountain bench surrounded by steeper terrain outside the playable area
- **Latitude:** roughly 3.5° N
- **Climate character:** cool, humid, rainy, weak annual temperature seasonality, strong rainfall seasonality, frequent cloud/fog influence

This is a fictional microsite, but it is geographically plausible. San Antonio contains forest fragments and ridge/hilltop terrain between roughly 1,800 and 2,200 m, and published research has sampled actual hilltops and small locally level areas within this landscape.

---

# 2. Why San Antonio / KM18 Is the Best Anchor

The San Antonio forest system is well studied and sits at the intersection of several climatic influences:

- moist Pacific air moving inland
- the Western Cordillera forcing that air upward
- cooling and condensation near the ridge
- the drier Cauca Valley lying to the east
- cloud forest beginning around the upper slopes

Published descriptions characterize San Antonio as a cloud-forest or very humid lower montane environment, with regular fog/low cloud formation and a strongly bimodal rainfall regime.

The site is more appropriate for the game than using the entire Valle del Cauca as one climate zone because the valley floor, lower Yotoco forest, KM18 ridge, and high Farallones all differ substantially in temperature and rainfall.

---

# 3. Elevation and Climate Gradient

The surrounding region changes quickly with altitude.

## San Antonio / KM18

Published values include:

- **1,800–2,200 m elevation**
- **15°C annual mean temperature** and **3,000 mm annual rainfall** in one San Antonio study
- **16.7°C mean temperature** and **91.3% annual mean relative humidity** in another San Antonio cloud-forest study
- approximately **12–20°C** as a reported annual/local temperature range in another description
- **1,967 mm mean annual rainfall** in one San Antonio dataset
- **18°C mean annual temperature** and **2,000 mm annual rainfall** at La Florida, a site within the San Antonio–KM18 landscape at **1,995–2,070 m**

These values differ because the studies use different microsites, elevations, periods, and methods. The disagreement is useful rather than fatal: it shows how much local topography matters in this mountain landscape.

## Yotoco Forest Reserve

Yotoco is lower and warmer than the recommended game plateau.

Published values include:

- roughly **1,200–1,700 m**
- monthly mean temperatures around **21°C**
- annual mean relative humidity around **75%**
- annual rainfall around **1,500–1,790 mm**
- two wet periods and two drier periods

Yotoco is useful as a regional comparison, but it should not be used as the exact temperature baseline for a fictional 1,900–2,000 m cloud-forest plateau.

# 4. Recommended Game Climate Baseline

For the fictional plateau at roughly 1,900–2,000 m:

## Temperature

Recommended climatological center:

- **Annual mean target: ~17°C**
- defensible long-term range for the site's mean: roughly **16–18°C**
- annual month-to-month temperature changes should be relatively small

This is a game baseline chosen from the published San Antonio values, not a claim that a single weather station reports exactly 17.0°C.

## Rainfall

Recommended long-term annual rainfall target:

- **~2,200–2,500 mm/year**

This sits between published San Antonio estimates of approximately 1,967 mm, 2,000 mm, and 3,000 mm, while remaining well within the 2,000–4,000 mm climatic range reported for very humid lower montane forest at San Antonio.

## Relative Humidity

Recommended climatological baseline:

- **high humidity almost all year**
- game target centered around **~90% RH**
- ordinary range could commonly occupy roughly **80–100% RH**
- San Antonio research has reported an **annual mean of 91.3% RH**

Humidity should therefore feel persistently high rather than behave like a dry temperate climate.

# 5. The Real Seasonal Pattern

This region should **not** use Spring, Summer, Fall, and Winter as thermal seasons.

At only a few degrees north of the equator, the major annual cycle is precipitation, not temperature.

The broad pattern is:

1. **Less-rainy period:** December–February
2. **First wetter period:** March–May
3. **Second less-rainy period:** June–August
4. **Second wetter period:** September–November

San Antonio-specific sources particularly identify rainfall peaks in:

- **April–May**
- **October–November**

One San Antonio source states that approximately **47% of annual rainfall** occurs during those four peak months.

The bimodal pattern is consistent with the twice-yearly migration of the tropical rain belt / Intertropical Convergence Zone across Colombia.

Important wording:

**"Less rainy" is better than "dry."**

This is still a cloud forest. Even in a lower-rainfall month, humidity, fog, wet soil, cloud cover, and intermittent rain can remain common.

- Municipality: Cali

- the exact probability at the fictional plateau may reasonably be somewhat wetter
- do not pretend the Pichindé numbers are exact measurements from the fictional plateau

# 7. What the Monthly Rain Pattern Means

## December–February: First Less-Rainy Period

Nearby Pichindé still averages roughly:

- January: 9.6 rainy days
- February: 9.2 rainy days
- December: 11.3 rainy days

This is not a drought.

Game-world feel:

- rain remains possible
- cloud and fog remain common
- soils can remain moist
- flooding risk is reduced relative to peak wet months
- clearer periods may become more frequent

## March–May: First Wetter Period

Rain frequency rises sharply:

- March: 13.7 rainy days
- April: 16.2 rainy days
- May: 16.1 rainy days

April and May are among the core San Antonio rainfall peaks.

Game-world feel:

- repeated rain events
- high soil saturation
- greater tunnel infiltration/flood risk
- fog and cloud common
- short dry intervals between wet events

## June–August: Second Less-Rainy Period

Rain frequency drops:

- June: 9.7 rainy days
- July: 7.9 rainy days
- August: 5.9 rainy days

August is the strongest local minimum in the official Pichindé rainy-day normal.

Game-world feel:

- longer rain-free stretches
- less saturated near-surface soil
- reduced flood frequency
- greater daytime solar heating when clouds break
- humidity can still remain high because this is cloud forest

## September–November: Second Wetter Period

Rain returns:

- September: 9.5 rainy days
- October: 13.8 rainy days
- November: 13.9 rainy days

San Antonio literature identifies October–November as the second major rainfall peak.

Game-world feel:

- renewed soil saturation
- repeated rain and storm windows
- stronger flood risk
- wet leaf litter and active decomposition
- greater persistent moisture

# 8. Rainfall Amount vs. Rainy-Day Frequency

These are not the same thing.

A month may have:

- many light-rain days
- fewer heavy-rain days
- similar rain-day frequency but different total rainfall

Therefore the game should not model rainfall using a single binary "rain chance" alone.

A better climate model separates:

1. **Chance that precipitation occurs**
2. **Intensity of the event**
3. **Duration of the event**
4. **Antecedent soil/water saturation**

This allows two wet months to feel different even if both are rainy.

---

# 9. Temperature Seasonality

The annual temperature cycle should be weak.

Unlike a temperate climate:

- January should not be "winter cold"
- July should not be "summer hot"
- the wet/drier rainfall cycle is much stronger than annual thermal seasonality

For the fictional plateau, the temperature system should remain centered near the same annual baseline throughout the year.

Recommended design direction:

- seasonal/monthly mean shifts generally **small**
- ordinary month-to-month target changes perhaps on the order of **~0–2°C**
- wet periods can run slightly cooler during the day because of cloud cover
- less-rainy periods can permit somewhat stronger daytime warming under clearer skies
- cloudier nights can remain relatively mild because cloud cover reduces radiative cooling
- clearer nights can cool somewhat more

The precise hourly temperature curve below is a **game model**, not a directly observed hourly San Antonio station series.

# 10. Proposed Daily Temperature Model

The strongest temperature change in the game should occur through the **Day/Night cycle**, not the "season."

## Recommended game target

At the fictional plateau:

- climatological center: **17°C**
- common cool-night target: roughly **13–16°C**
- common daytime target: roughly **17–21°C**
- occasional cooler or warmer events can exceed this ordinary band

Do not hard-code Night itself as an ant debuff.

Instead:

**world temperature changes → organisms respond according to their biology**

## Proposed daily curve

A smooth model could use:

- **Night minimum:** near the late-night / pre-dawn portion
- **Morning:** gradual warming
- **Early-to-mid afternoon:** daily maximum
- **Late afternoon:** cloud/fog can reduce or reverse heating
- **Evening:** gradual cooling

This matters because San Antonio literature specifically notes frequent **afternoon fog and low cloud** produced by moist Pacific air condensing near the ridge.

---

# 11. Cloud Cover and Fog

Cloud/fog should be treated as a meaningful climate state even if it is initially benign.

San Antonio literature describes:

- high relative humidity
- moist Pacific air reaching the ridge
- condensation on the mountain crest
- fog/low cloud commonly covering the forest, especially in the afternoon

Possible world-state variables:

- Clear
- Partly Cloudy
- Cloudy
- Fog / Low Cloud
- Rain
- Thunderstorm

These do not need to affect ants directly.

They can initially influence only:

- displayed temperature
- humidity
- rainfall probability
- lighting/ambience
- future surface visibility
- future evaporation or soil drying

---

# 12. Humidity Mechanic

Humidity can exist now even if it has no biological consequences yet.

## Suggested variable

Track:

- **Relative Humidity: 0–100%**

Recommended baseline for this plateau:

- ordinary cloud-forest state: **~85–95%**
- saturated fog/rain: **95–100%**
- unusually clear/drier daytime period: perhaps **~75–85%**
- long-term target centered near **~90%**

This is consistent with San Antonio's reported mean RH of roughly 91.3% and with lower-elevation regional sites showing lower averages.

## Benign first implementation

For now, humidity can:

- update continuously
- appear in weather/debug UI
- respond to time of day
- respond to rainfall
- respond to cloud/fog
- respond to the annual rainfall phase

It does **not** need to affect ants yet.

Future systems may eventually use it for:

- desiccation
- egg/larval microclimate
- fungi
- isopods
- mites
- earthworm surface behavior
- soil moisture
- evaporation
- Evolution traits

---

# 13. Proposed Humidity Logic

Conceptual model only:

```text
humidity_target =
    monthly_climate_baseline
    + night_adjustment
    + cloud_adjustment
    + rain_adjustment
    + fog_adjustment
    - clear_sun_adjustment

humidity smoothly approaches humidity_target
```

Example conceptual behavior:

- Night: RH tends upward
- Fog: RH approaches saturation
- Rain: RH becomes very high
- Clear midday: RH falls somewhat
- Wet-period month: baseline is higher
- August-like less-rainy month: baseline is lower, but still humid

Do not use these as direct ant modifiers.

---

# 14. Proposed Temperature Logic

Conceptual model:

```text
monthly_mean = climate_table[current_climate_month]

time_of_day_offset = smooth_daily_temperature_curve(time_of_day)

cloud_effect = current_cloud_state.temperature_modifier

weather_effect = active_weather.temperature_modifier

random_weather_noise = slowly varying small anomaly

target_temperature =
    monthly_mean
    + time_of_day_offset
    + cloud_effect
    + weather_effect
    + random_weather_noise

temperature smoothly approaches target_temperature
```

Important principles:

- no abrupt temperature jumps at Day/Night transition
- no direct "Night penalty"
- cloud/rain can suppress daytime warming
- clear conditions can increase daily thermal range
- the annual average changes only slightly

# 15. Recommended Climate Calendar

The game can retain a "season" concept, but it should represent the regional rainfall cycle.

Recommended climate phases:

## Phase 1 — First Less-Rainy Period
**December–February**

## Phase 2 — First Wetter Period
**March–May**

## Phase 3 — Second Less-Rainy Period
**June–August**

## Phase 4 — Second Wetter Period
**September–November**

Internally, it is even better to track **12 climate months** because IDEAM gives us real monthly rain-frequency differences.

That allows:

- April to be wetter than March
- August to be distinctly drier than June
- December to remain wetter than July/August even though both belong to "less-rainy" phases

# 16. Recommended Game-Time Mapping

The existing Day/Night cycle is:

- Day: **15 real minutes**
- Night: **15 real minutes**
- One full game day: **30 real minutes**

There are two reasonable climate-clock approaches.

## Option A — One Game Day = One Climate Day

Scientifically intuitive, but a full 365-day climate year would take:

- **182.5 real hours**

This may be too long for most runs.

## Option B — Compressed Climate Calendar

Recommended.

Keep the 30-minute physical Day/Night cycle, but allow climate months to advance after a configurable number of game days.

Example:

- **3 game days per climate month**
- 12 climate months = 36 game days
- one climate year = **18 real hours**

Or:

- **5 game days per climate month**
- one climate year = 60 game days
- one climate year = **30 real hours**

This preserves the real order and relative monthly climate while making the full annual pattern observable during a long sandbox run.

The exact compression rate remains a game-design choice.

# 17. Converting Real Rainy-Day Normals into Game Rain Chances

If the game treats each full 30-minute Day/Night cycle as one climate day, the IDEAM rainy-day frequency can become a first-order rainfall probability.

Examples from Pichindé:

- January: ~31.0% of days
- April: ~54.0%
- August: ~19.0%
- November: ~46.3%

For the higher fictional cloud-forest plateau, these may be used as a **minimum/reference curve** rather than exact values.

## Better algorithm

At the start of each game day:

```text
base_rain_probability = monthly_rain_day_probability
site_wetness_adjustment = fictional_plateau_adjustment
large_scale_weather_anomaly = random slowly-changing modifier

daily_rain_probability =
    clamp(
        base_rain_probability
        + site_wetness_adjustment
        + large_scale_weather_anomaly
    )
```

Then, if that day is a "rain day," determine one or more actual rain events within the 30-minute cycle.

This is more realistic than checking the same arbitrary rain probability at every Day↔Night boundary.

# 18. Proposed Rain Event Logic

A climate day can first determine whether precipitation is likely.

If rain is selected:

1. Pick event type:
   - light rain
   - moderate rain
   - heavy rain
   - thunderstorm

2. Pick event start time:
   - morning
   - afternoon
   - evening
   - night

3. Pick duration.

4. Pick intensity.

5. Feed the resulting water into the existing hydrology.

Conceptually:

```text
if random() < daily_rain_probability:
    rain_day = true

    event_count = choose_event_count(month, wetness_state)

    for each event:
        intensity = weighted_random(light, moderate, heavy, thunderstorm)
        duration = sample_duration(intensity)
        start_time = sample_start_time(climate_context)
```

This does not require all rainfall to produce an underground flood.

The existing **15% per-minute underground flood event concept** can either remain as a separate infiltration hazard or eventually be replaced by a more physical link between rainfall intensity, saturation, and infiltration.

That decision should be made in the Day/Weather design document, not silently here.

---

# 19. Soil Saturation as a Future Bridge Between Climate and Flooding

A useful future world variable would be:

**Soil Saturation: 0–100%**

Possible behavior:

- Rain raises saturation.
- Heavy rain raises it rapidly.
- Long rain-free periods lower it.
- High humidity slows drying.
- clear/warm periods increase drying.
- cloud/fog slows drying.

Then underground flood risk can depend partly on existing saturation.

Example concept:

```text
infiltration_risk =
    rain_intensity
    * soil_saturation_factor
    * local_terrain_factor
```

This would make repeated storms more dangerous than an isolated rain shower without needing an arbitrary "wet season damage bonus."

Not required for the first version.

---

# 20. Thunderstorm Logic

Thunderstorms should be a subset of wet weather, not an unrelated season.

Possible logic:

```text
if rain_event_generated:
    thunderstorm_chance =
        monthly_storm_baseline
        + atmospheric_instability_modifier
```

A thunderstorm can then use the already-designed mechanics:

- lightning `.ogg`
- chance of lightning strike
- surface breach
- strong temporary water inflow
- normal hydrology determines spread

The existing **20–30 Level-7 tile-equivalent** inflow concept remains a game mechanic, not a climatological measurement.

---

# 21. Heavy Wind Logic

Heavy Wind does not need to be directly tied to a "season buff."

Possible weather-state logic:

- can occur independently
- can accompany Rain
- can accompany Thunderstorm
- probability can vary with generated weather systems

Its existing cave-in mechanic can remain:

- periodic collapse checks
- 2x2 or 3x3 collapse concept
- fog/visibility updates after terrain recloses

Exact probabilities remain a gameplay question.

---

# 22. Earthquake Logic

Earthquakes are **not climate or weather**.

They should be categorized as a separate environmental/geological event.

This is important scientifically.

The existing earthquake mechanic can remain:

- rare
- major colony-area cave-in
- open terrain can collapse
- built tiles can be destroyed
- entities can be crushed
- Queen protected from direct cave-in death

But its probability should not be driven by:

- rain month
- wet season
- humidity
- Day/Night

unless a future geological mechanic explicitly establishes a separate reason.

# 24. Creature Response Architecture

Do not code:

```text
if night:
    all_creatures.speed *= 0.8
```

Use:

```text
world climate state
        ↓
species / creature biology
        ↓
behavior response
```

Examples:

- Hercules Beetle may use Day/Night because adults are crepuscular/nocturnal.
- Isopods may eventually respond to humidity/exposure.
- Earthworms may eventually respond to surface moisture and Night for emergence.
- Root Aphids may continue feeding across both Day and Night.
- generalized ants should not receive a universal Night modifier.

This keeps the game scientifically extensible.

---

# 25. Climate Variation Between Years

Real rainfall is not identical every year.

A future system can vary:

- onset strength of wet periods
- total rain
- storm intensity
- length of rain-free runs

Without changing the basic bimodal pattern.

Conceptual model:

```text
annual_wetness_anomaly = random_normal(mean=0, small_variance)

monthly_rain_probability =
    climatological_month_probability
    + annual_wetness_anomaly
    + short_term_weather_variation
```

This prevents every run from having an identical climate script.

---

# 26. Climate Extremes

The climate model should distinguish:

## Normal variation
- ordinary rain
- fog
- clear intervals
- ordinary warm/cool days

## Weather events
- heavy rain
- thunderstorms
- heavy wind

## Geological events
- earthquake

This prevents "Earthquake" from incorrectly being treated as a type of weather.

# 27. Suggested Initial Climate Mechanics

For a first climate implementation, without overbuilding:

### Track immediately
- 30-minute Day/Night cycle
- 12 climate months
- 4 rainfall phases
- temperature
- relative humidity
- cloud/fog state
- rain events
- thunderstorm state
- wind state
- earthquake as separate environmental event

### Temperature
- smooth daily curve
- annual center near 17°C
- small month-to-month changes
- cloud/weather modifies daily range

### Humidity
- mostly 80–100%
- centered near ~90%
- higher at Night, during fog, and during rain
- lower during clearer/drier daytime conditions
- no ant effect required initially

### Rain
- monthly probability derived from real regional rainy-day normals
- event intensity/duration generated separately
- wet periods increase repeated rain/flood opportunities naturally

### Seasons
Replace temperate seasons with:
- First Less-Rainy
- First Wetter
- Second Less-Rainy
- Second Wetter

Or expose actual month names while using the four phases only as internal labels.

# 29. Scientific Confidence Map

## High confidence
- San Antonio/KM18 is a real Andean cloud-forest landscape.
- cloud forest occurs at the target elevation.
- annual temperatures are cool and comparatively stable.
- annual rainfall is high.
- humidity is high.
- rainfall is bimodal.
- April–May and October–November are major wet peaks.
- June–August is a pronounced lower-rainfall interval.
- afternoon fog/low cloud is characteristic.
- nearby IDEAM Pichindé shows a strong monthly rainy-day cycle.
- Earthquake is geological, not weather.

## Moderate confidence / site interpolation
- fictional plateau mean around 17°C
- annual rainfall target around 2,200–2,500 mm
- RH centered around 90%
- fictional plateau somewhat wetter than Pichindé

These are reasonable syntheses from nearby published San Antonio measurements rather than one exact station record.

## Game abstraction
- exact hourly temperature curve
- exact humidity curve
- climate-month compression
- event duration
- underground flood chance
- lightning breach water volume
- weather overlap rules
- soil saturation system

These should be tuned for gameplay while remaining consistent with the real climate.

# 31. Core Game-Design Rule Going Forward

The climate system should follow:

**Real regional climate → world state → organism-specific response**

Not:

**Calendar label → arbitrary ant buff/debuff**

Examples:

- Wet month increases actual rain probability.
- Rain raises actual water and saturation.
- Temperature changes smoothly through the day.
- Humidity rises under rain/fog.
- A creature only responds if its biology supports responding.
- Evolution modifies how the ant lineage tolerates environmental conditions.

This gives the game a real climatic foundation while preserving room for simulation, ecology, and future Evolution systems.
